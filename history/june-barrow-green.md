# June Barrow-Green

## Why this is a seed

Barrow-Green's work is unusually useful for a `math.HO` repository because it stays close to mathematics while reconstructing the documentary and institutional conditions around it. Her strongest first-pass nodes are Poincaré's three-body work, British/Cambridge mathematics, applied mathematics, mathematical instruments/models, and the historical visibility of women mathematicians.

## Major strands

### Poincaré, the Oscar II prize, and the three-body problem

Her 1993 Open University thesis became *Poincaré and the Three Body Problem* (AMS/LMS, 1997). The work reconstructs the prize competition, the error in Poincaré's original memoir, the substantially revised published memoir, and its place in the development of qualitative dynamics.

The historical point is not merely “Poincaré anticipated chaos.” The source trail makes the revision process itself mathematically important: the correction of the error forced new geometry/dynamics into view.

Immediate historical nodes include Henri Poincaré, Gösta Mittag-Leffler, Lars Edvard Phragmén, Hugo Gyldén, George William Hill, E. T. Whittaker, Jacques Hadamard, George D. Birkhoff, Karl Sundman, Marston Morse, and the later KAM line.

### Cambridge and British mathematics

Representative nodes from the ORO list:

- “Geometry at Cambridge, 1863–1940” (with Jeremy Gray, 2006).
- “Wranglers in exile” (2011).
- work on George Gabriel Stokes's mathematical education;
- Robert Leslie Ellis;
- Augustus Love;
- Cambridge mathematicians during the First World War.

This branch should be treated institutionally as well as biographically: Tripos culture, research migration, war work, teaching, and the boundary between “pure” and “applied” mathematics.

### Applied mathematics, computing, and instruments

Important nodes include:

- Karl Pearson and Ministry of Munitions computing;
- Olaus Henrici and harmonic analysers;
- surface models;
- Ronald Ross and Hilda Hudson on epidemic mathematics;
- George Birkhoff's dynamics program.

This branch is useful because “computation” appears before electronic computers as organized human and mechanical mathematical work.

### Women and mathematical institutions

Her work on Philippa Fawcett, women at Cambridge, British women mathematicians during WWI, and the historical gender gap belongs in the mathematical-institutional history rather than as an isolated side topic.
