# Jeremy Gray

## Why this is a seed

Gray's corpus offers a broad internal history of modern mathematics: geometry, complex analysis, differential equations, algebra, Hilbert, Poincaré, mathematical modernism, and the transformation of nineteenth-century mathematical practice.

## Major mathematical-history lines

### Geometry

- *Ideas of Space*.
- *János Bolyai, Non-Euclidean Geometry, and the Nature of Space*.
- *Worlds Out of Nothing*.
- work on Desargues and nineteenth-century geometry.

This branch should cross-reference Papadopoulos only when a citation edge is verified; at present the overlap is thematic: non-Euclidean geometry, Klein, Riemann, group actions, and changing notions of space.

### Differential equations, groups, and complex analysis

*Linear Differential Equations and Group Theory from Riemann to Poincaré* tracks the convergence of differential equations, group theory, non-Euclidean geometry, the Riemann–Hilbert problem, uniformization, Picard–Vessiot theory, and hypergeometric equations.

*Hidden Harmony* (with Umberto Bottazzini) follows the rise of complex function theory.

### Hilbert and mathematical modernism

*The Hilbert Challenge* and *Plato's Ghost* treat mathematics not simply as a sequence of solved problems but as a changing professional/intellectual culture: axiomatization, set theory, definitions, proof standards, foundations, philosophy, and the public image of mathematics.

### Poincaré

*Henri Poincaré: A Scientific Biography* is a natural cross-seed node with Barrow-Green. Their emphases differ: Barrow-Green's three-body work is a close archival/mathematical study of one episode; Gray's biography follows Poincaré across a much larger mathematical and scientific range.

### Broad nineteenth-century mathematical textbooks

- *The Real and the Complex* — analysis.
- *A History of Abstract Algebra*.
- *Change and Variations* — differential equations and calculus of variations.

These are ideal spider hubs because each necessarily compresses a large earlier primary-source graph.
