## What the review does

Gottlieb treats Barrow-Green's archival reconstruction as an occasion to discuss mathematical practice rather than merely summarising the book.

Four strands matter for this repository:

1. **Error and mathematical character.** Poincaré's response to the serious error in the prize memoir is set against a modern argument about inadequate proof and mathematical responsibility.
2. **Mathematicians versus dynamical astronomers.** The disagreement over divergent/convergent series is presented as a difference in what counts as a useful mathematical object: rigorous convergence versus finite truncations with practical error control.
3. **Sundman's solution.** Gottlieb emphasizes the historical oddity that a globally convergent series solution can count as a “solution” while contributing relatively little practical or qualitative understanding.
4. **Pure mathematics versus mathematical practice.** He turns the history into an explicit reflection on mathematics as exact understanding and mathematics as a tool used in science and engineering.

This is a useful secondary node because it shows how Barrow-Green's history was immediately recruited into a live 1990s debate about rigor, proof, physics, and mathematical culture.

## Verified outgoing reference

The review has one formal bibliography entry:

1. Arthur Jaffe and Frank Quinn, “Theoretical Mathematics: Toward a Cultural Synthesis of Mathematics and Theoretical Physics,” *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society* (N.S.) 29 (1993), no. 1, 1–13.

Gottlieb invokes Jaffe–Quinn's discussion of insufficient proof, including their treatment of Poincaré, and uses Barrow-Green's archival evidence to challenge the moral/historical inference one might draw from that discussion.
