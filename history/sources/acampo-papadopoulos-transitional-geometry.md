## Mathematical note

The paper constructs a one-parameter family joining constant negative, zero, and positive curvature geometries. Points, lines, distances, angles, triangles, area, curvature, and trigonometric relations are organized so they vary coherently from hyperbolic through Euclidean to spherical/elliptic geometry.

The conceptual device is group-theoretic: each geometry is treated through transformation groups/homogeneous spaces in the Erlangen-program spirit. The Euclidean fiber requires a coherence condition because naïvely degenerating the quadratic form makes the limiting orthogonal group too large.

Historically, the authors connect their construction to Klein's nineteenth-century transition ideas and distinguish it from modern three-dimensional geometric transitions associated with cone-manifolds, Thurston geometries, and hyperbolic/anti-de Sitter limits.
