## Why this source entered the graph

Daniel Henry Gottlieb's 1999 review of June Barrow-Green's *Poincaré and the Three Body Problem* has exactly one formal reference: this paper. Gottlieb invokes it because Jaffe and Quinn use Poincaré as part of a warning about speculative mathematical work and incomplete proof.

The citation therefore creates a real historical edge:

**Barrow-Green → Gottlieb review → Jaffe–Quinn → twentieth-century debate over speculation, proof, physics, and mathematical publication.**

## Argument, without adopting it

Jaffe and Quinn distinguish an intuitive/speculative stage of mathematical work from a proof-oriented stage. They propose “theoretical mathematics” as a label for the former and argue that the distinction should be made explicit in publication and citation practices.

Their analogy is that proof in mathematics has a reliability-testing role somewhat analogous to experiment in natural science. They are particularly concerned with mathematical ideas entering from theoretical physics and with results circulating rapidly before complete proofs are available.

The paper is itself part of a controversy, not a neutral description of mathematical practice. It should be read together with its response literature rather than treated as the repository's position.

## Structure of the bibliography

The references deliberately mix mathematical history, algebraic geometry, mathematical/theoretical physics, chaos/fractals, biography, and arithmetic geometry. This breadth reflects the paper's cultural argument rather than a narrowly technical theorem lineage.
