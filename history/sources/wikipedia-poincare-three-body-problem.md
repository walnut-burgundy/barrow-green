## Mathematical/historical route exposed by the article

The article follows Barrow-Green's monograph from the classical three-body problem through Poincaré's 1889 prize memoir, the error found before publication, the revised 1890 memoir, and the later *Méthodes nouvelles de la mécanique céleste*.

It also exposes the later mathematical branches treated by the book:

- singularities and regularisation: Painlevé, von Zeipel, Levi-Civita, Chazy, McGehee, Saari, Xia;
- stability: Lyapunov;
- numerical/periodic-orbit work: Darwin, Moulton, Strömgren;
- convergent-series work: Bisconcini and Sundman;
- qualitative dynamics: Hadamard, Birkhoff, Morse, Melnikov;
- KAM: Kolmogorov, Arnold, Moser.

These names are thematic children until exact source-level citation edges are recovered from Barrow-Green's own bibliography.

## Reception map

The page uses the reviews for more than a rating. The reviewers disagree about emphasis and mathematical adequacy:

- Chambers, Mawhin, Duda, Lewis, and Gottlieb are used to support the book's value, accessibility, documentation, or importance.
- Vickers is used for the warning that the mathematical core may require outside material.
- Diacu recommends the book but raises substantive objections, including the omission of Qiudong Wang's generalisation of Sundman's series, some missed internal connections in Poincaré's work, and translation problems.

That disagreement is worth keeping intact. It should not be collapsed into “the book was well received.”
