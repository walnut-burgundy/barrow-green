# Athanase Papadopoulos

## Why this is a seed

Papadopoulos differs from the other seeds because a large part of his output is active mathematics rather than history alone. That is useful here: his historical writing often traces a live mathematical structure backward through the sources.

## Mathematical corpus

His stated fields include low-dimensional geometry/topology, metric geometry, Riemann surfaces, complex analysis, combinatorial group theory, dynamical systems, history/philosophy of mathematics, and mathematical/music history.

Early work forms a dense Teichmüller/Thurston/mapping-class-group graph: pseudo-Anosov maps, measured foliations, Thurston boundary, Weil–Petersson structure, earthquakes, hyperbolic groups, and asymmetric metrics.

## History from inside geometry

### Klein, Lie, and geometry as transformation theory

The 2015 volume *Sophus Lie and Felix Klein: The Erlangen Program and Its Impact in Mathematics and Physics*, edited with Lizhen Ji, treats groups and group actions as the organizing language connecting geometry and physics.

The first fully spidered child in this repository is:

- [Norbert A'Campo & Athanase Papadopoulos, “Transitional Geometry”](sources/acampo-papadopoulos-transitional-geometry.md)

That paper explicitly cites Klein's 1871/1873 non-Euclidean papers, Riemann, Weyl, Dieudonné, Lobachevsky, Stillwell, and modern geometric-transition work.

### Riemann and predecessors

“Looking backward: From Euler to Riemann” traces Riemann's work through complex functions, elliptic/Abelian integrals, hypergeometric series, zeta, topology, differential geometry, integration, and concepts of space, with Euler as a recurrent predecessor.

This is valuable as a history-of-ideas paper written by someone whose own mathematics lies in descendant fields.

### Teichmüller theory as a historical chain

Papadopoulos's historical surveys track the path Riemann → Klein/Poincaré → Brouwer/Weyl → Teichmüller → Weil → Ahlfors/Bers and later Thurston. This branch is especially useful for preventing the history of Teichmüller space from being falsely collapsed onto one eponym.

### *Geometry in History* (2019)

Edited with S. G. Dani. Publisher metadata describes the book as a collection in which historical origins, development, and current research are kept together. This should be spidered chapter by chapter rather than as one undifferentiated bibliography.
