## Verified relations among the seeds

- **Gray → Barrow-Green:** Gray supervised Barrow-Green's Open University PhD.
- **Barrow-Green ↔ Gray:** coauthors of *The History of Mathematics: A Source-Based Approach* and of work on geometry at Cambridge.
- **Buchwald ↔ Gray:** editorial collaboration on *Archive for History of Exact Sciences*.
- **Lützen ↔ Buchwald:** connected through the Springer/Archimedes history-of-mathematics-and-physics editorial world; this edge is institutional/editorial, not a claim that every work cites the other.
- **Lützen ↔ Gray:** both appear as authors of the 2022 methodological reply “A Question of Fundamental Methodology.”
- **Papadopoulos → Klein/Riemann/Thurston traditions:** directly verified in historical/mathematical papers; this is the strongest first-pass bridge from the seed set into active geometry.

## Mathematical-history clusters

### Poincaré, dynamics, and celestial mechanics

Barrow-Green's corpus makes the 1889/1890 three-body memoir a hub linking Poincaré, Mittag-Leffler, Phragmén, Hill, Gyldén, Whittaker, Hadamard, Birkhoff, Morse, Sundman, and later KAM theory. Gray independently makes Poincaré a central figure through his scientific biography and his work on differential equations, groups, geometry, and complex function theory.

The recursion now has two secondary source nodes beneath the Barrow-Green seed:

- [Wikipedia's book article](sources/wikipedia-poincare-three-body-problem.md), which exposes eight reception references and keeps their disagreements attributed;
- [Daniel Henry Gottlieb's 1999 review](sources/gottlieb-review-poincare-three-body-problem.md), whose sole formal citation leads to Jaffe–Quinn's 1993 debate over proof and theoretical mathematics.

This branch therefore already connects the nineteenth-century three-body story to a late-twentieth-century argument over rigor and mathematical culture.

### Geometry as a changing language

Gray follows Euclidean/non-Euclidean geometry, projective geometry, Riemann, Bolyai, Hilbert, and Poincaré. Papadopoulos supplies a complementary line in which the historical material remains close to current geometry: Klein geometries, Lie groups, Teichmüller space, Thurston geometry, metric geometry, and deformation/transition of geometric structures.

The first fully enumerated bibliography node is [A'Campo–Papadopoulos, “Transitional Geometry”](sources/acampo-papadopoulos-transitional-geometry.md).

### Analysis, generalized functions, and differential equations

Lützen's research program begins with the changing concept of function and the prehistory of distributions, then passes through Liouville, Sturm–Liouville theory, differential equations, spectral ideas, and the relation of pure mathematics to mechanics and physics. Gray's histories of analysis and differential equations intersect this cluster from a broader nineteenth-century viewpoint.

### Electromagnetism, optics, and mechanics

Buchwald's books on Maxwellian electrodynamics, wave optics, and Hertz focus on the interaction of mathematical formalism, experiment, instruments, and tacit scientific practice. Lützen reaches Hertz from the history of mechanics rather than electrodynamics. That makes Hertz a particularly useful cross-seed comparison node.

### Institutions and mathematical communities

Barrow-Green's work on Acta Mathematica, Cambridge, the LMS, war work, mathematical education, and gender exposes the institutional machinery around mathematics. Buchwald's Dibner/Caltech editorial work and the journal/series connections among Buchwald, Gray, and Lützen expose another institutional layer.
