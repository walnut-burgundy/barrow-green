## Core books and mathematical themes

### *From Maxwell to Microphysics* (1985)

Electromagnetic theory in the last quarter of the nineteenth century. The associated article trail includes Maxwellian electrodynamics, Oliver Heaviside, Joseph Larmor, the Hall effect, and the changing continuum/field/electron picture.

### *The Rise of the Wave Theory of Light* (1989)

A mathematically detailed study of optical theory and experiment in the early nineteenth century. Fresnel, diffraction, polarization, Arago, and the interaction between physical effects and mathematical formalism form the natural source graph.

### *The Creation of Scientific Effects: Heinrich Hertz and Electric Waves* (1994)

The publisher description and contents make the method explicit: reconstruct Hertz's laboratory and theoretical world from lab notes, published papers, and unpublished manuscripts.

The book is especially valuable beside Lützen's Hertz book. Lützen asks how mechanics was rebuilt in geometric form; Buchwald asks how Hertz's electrodynamic effects and experimental objects were made and interpreted.

The publisher's contents expose explicit mathematical appendices on Helmholtzian electrodynamics, potentials, polarization, propagation, instability, quasi-field theory, and Poincaré/Bertrand.

### Newton, chronology, and ancient evidence

Later work with Mordechai Feingold moves into Newton, chronology, and the historical use of ancient evidence. The current CV lists *The Winding Trail to Newton's Principia Mathematica* as in press in 2026; because publication state may change, this node should be rechecked before treating the final edition as fixed.
